## Portfolio Artifact Submission – CS-250  

### Sprint Review & Retrospective  
The **Sprint Review & Retrospective** reflects on how Agile and Scrum practices were applied during the SNHU Travel project. This report covers roles, user stories, communication strategies, handling interruptions, and evaluating the Agile process. It shows how Agile allowed us to adapt quickly to changes (like adding a currency converter mid-sprint) and how tools such as Jira, Confluence, and Slack supported collaboration.

## Essential Questions  

### How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating “user stories” help with this?  
I interpret user needs by breaking them down into clear, actionable requirements that reflect what the end user values most. Writing user stories helps me stay focused on the “who, what, and why” behind each feature. Instead of just building functionality, I’m building solutions that align with user goals.  

### How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?  
My approach is iterative and feedback-driven. I like to start small, test quickly, and adjust as I go. Agile processes such as sprint planning, daily stand-ups, and retrospectives allow me to adapt to changes and continuously improve both the product and the team’s workflow. In the future, I want to keep using Agile practices like backlog refinement and user story mapping because they make development more flexible and user-focused.  

### What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?  
Being a good team member means contributing not only technical skills but also communication, accountability, and support for others. It’s about collaborating, listening, and stepping in where needed to keep the project moving forward. A strong team member balances individual responsibility with team goals and helps create an environment where everyone can succeed.
